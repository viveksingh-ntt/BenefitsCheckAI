# Referrals
Your primary care doctor must give you a referral before you see most specialists. A referral names the specialist or practice, the service and the number of visits. Referrals are valid for 90 days from the date they are issued and cover only the visits listed. If you need more visits, ask your PCP for a new referral. You do not need a referral for preventive care, emergency care, urgent care, behavioral health visits or OB/GYN visits with a network provider.

# Seeing a specialist without a referral
If you see a specialist without a valid referral, the visit is not covered and you may have to pay the full cost. Referrals cannot be issued after the visit.

# Prior authorization
Some services need approval from Harbor before you receive them. Your treating doctor requests prior authorization. Examples are inpatient admissions, advanced imaging (MRI, CT, PET), Mohs surgery, and specialty drugs. Office visits, including dermatology exams with a referral, do not need prior authorization. A decision is made within 15 calendar days, or 72 hours for urgent requests.

# Medically necessary
Medically necessary means services a doctor provides to diagnose or treat an illness, injury or its symptoms that meet accepted standards of medicine. Cosmetic services are not medically necessary.

# Appeals
If a claim or prior authorization is denied, you can appeal within 180 days of the notice. Call Member Services or submit the appeal form in the portal. You'll get a decision within 30 days, or 72 hours for urgent appeals.
